# Assumptions and limitations

What timegraft takes for granted, and where it is known to be weak. Evidence comes from the [first run on real data](results/2026-11-iberia.md) and the [tests of crown age dating](results/2026-11-dating.md). Each weakness links to the issue tracking it.

## Assumptions

- **The backbone is right.** Branching order above genus level comes from the Open Tree of Life synthesis and ages from TimeTree, and both are taken as given. Phylogenetic diversity is dominated by deep branches, so errors there matter more than errors within genera.
- **Genera are natural groups.** One tree is built per genus and grafted onto one tip. Genera that are not natural groups (not monophyletic) break this.
- **Barcodes are informative within a genus.** The genus is used as the default range in which barcode distances track evolutionary distance. The validation step can measure this per group.
- **A roughly constant rate of change within each genus.** Both default dating methods (mean path length, LSD2) assume clock-like evolution within a genus. Penalised likelihood, which does not, is available (`chronos`) but changed within-genus branch lengths little on the Iberian genera and is not the default ([results](results/2026-11-dating.md)).
- **A COI rate that gives a minimum age.** The age from the barcodes uses 0.0115 substitutions per site per million years per lineage (about 2.3% pairwise), within the range of rates calibrated on splits of a few million years. Because distances saturate, that age is treated as a lower bound of the crown age, and by default also used as the estimate. The rate also sets how far back placed sample sequences attach.
- **Crown ages are known only as a range.** The estimate within the range (the minimum, or the midpoint on a log scale) is a convention, not a measurement; diversity is reported at both ends of the range for that reason.
- **One representative per BIN** stands in for the variation within it.
- **The genetic code follows from the taxonomy.** The reading-frame check on COI uses a mitochondrial code per record, looked up from its genus, family, order, class or phylum (see [configuration](../technical/configuration.md#genetic-codes)). Records whose names match nothing get a default code, and misidentified records get the code of the wrong group.

## Known weaknesses

| Weakness | Evidence | Issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TimeTree covers marine invertebrates poorly, so many backbone nodes are interpolated, and even spacing tends to make shallow undated nodes too old | 261 of 779 backbone nodes dated directly in the Iberian run; *Hediste*/*Nereis* at 291 Ma in a small test | [#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| TimeTree crown ages are inflated for genera that are not natural groups. They are now only an upper bound on the age from the barcodes, so an inflated one does no harm, but it also gives no information | 43 of 305 TimeTree crown ages in the Iberian run are not below the stem age, and others are far above the age from the barcodes (*Haliclona* at 444 Ma); the bound applies in 134 genera | [#36](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/36) |
| Barcodes say little about the crown age. The mean root-to-tip distance of a genus grows far less than in proportion to its age, so an age from a constant rate is a minimum, increasingly too young for older genera | Over 262 genera with a TimeTree crown age, the median distance is 0.04 for crown ages below 5 Ma and 0.13 above 80 Ma. In half of all genera the largest distance within the genus equals the distance to the outgroup. Literature ages for *Gammarus*, *Niphargus* and *Daphnia* are 5 to 12 times the barcode minimum | [#18](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/18) |
| Rates cannot be calibrated on these data. A rate per class fitted to TimeTree crown ages came out two to seven times below published rates and pushed all genera towards one age; marine invertebrates have few fossils, so calibration points will stay scarce | 128 of the 262 genera with a usable TimeTree crown age are teleosts ([record](../../data/iberia/calibration/README.md)) | [#18](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/18), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| Some units do not belong in their genus (misidentification, contamination, or genera that are not natural groups). Screening sets them aside, but cannot tell the cases apart, and its thresholds are judgements | 1,294 of 24,882 Iberian units set aside (850 closer to another genus, 444 isolated); 12 units at least 95% identical to a unit of another order or class ([results](results/2026-11-screening.md)) | [#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38) |
| Where on its genus's stem a set-aside unit with no close match belongs is not known; halfway is a convention | Raises Faith's PD of samples with five units per genus by 8% | [#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38) |
| Literature crown ages can exceed the stem age in the backbone, and are then cut to 0.9 of it | *Gammarus* (61 Ma against a stem of 53 Ma) and *Niphargus* (87 Ma against 23 Ma) | [#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| The Open Tree of Life contains misplacements, which nothing corrects automatically | the gastropod *Onchidella* among the sea urchins, because OTT merged it with an echinoderm homonym | [#20](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/20) |
| A genus and its relatives often overlap in barcode distance, so some units end up on the wrong side of the root. Rooting now tolerates this, but the root of a genus remains uncertain where many units are misplaced | 534 of 1,046 genera have units on the wrong side (median 2); 579 units of 351 genera sit among the outgroup, some of them probably misidentified. The spread is now recorded per genus: 254 genera have more than one plausible root, and the crown age varies by more than 25% over those roots in 149 | [#19](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/19), [#34](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/34) |
| Placement by nearest neighbour is less accurate than likelihood-based placement, and its thresholds are not calibrated | at 80% identity the attachment age is already about 9 Ma | [#21](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/21) |
| BINs and UNITE species hypotheses are delimited differently | PD across markers is comparable in time but not in the granularity of the tips | [#15](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/15) |

## What timegraft is not

It is not a tree of life inferred from barcodes. The deep structure is borrowed from published syntheses; barcodes contribute only the shallow structure and the placement of samples. Results should be presented that way, with the provenance tables that record where every age came from.
