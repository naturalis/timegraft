# Assumptions and limitations

What timegraft takes for granted, and where it is known to be weak. Evidence comes from the [first run on real data](results/2026-11-iberia.md), the [tests of crown age dating](results/2026-11-dating.md) and the [benchmark of the consistency layer](../../data/iberia/consistency/benchmark/REPORT.md), whose full Iberian run of November 2026 is the source of the current numbers below. Each weakness links to the issue tracking it.

## Assumptions

- **The backbone is mostly right.** Branching order above genus level comes from the Open Tree of Life synthesis and ages from TimeTree. They are checked, not taken as given: genera whose placement disagrees with their classification and barcodes are moved before dating ([#49](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/49), [#50](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/50)), and conflicting ages are fitted together rather than accepted one by one ([#52](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/52)). But only gross errors (at class level or above) are caught, and a fitted age is only as good as the TimeTree estimates around it. Where TimeTree gives no age, a pure-birth model of the number of genera per clade fills the gap. Phylogenetic diversity is dominated by deep branches, so errors there matter more than errors within genera.
- **Genera are natural groups.** One tree is built per genus and grafted onto one tip. Genera that are not natural groups (not monophyletic) break this.
- **Barcodes are informative within a genus.** The genus is used as the default range in which barcode distances track evolutionary distance. The validation step can measure this per group.
- **A roughly constant rate of change within each genus.** Both default dating methods (mean path length, LSD2) assume clock-like evolution within a genus. Penalised likelihood, which does not, is available (`chronos`) but changed within-genus branch lengths little on the Iberian genera and is not the default ([results](results/2026-11-dating.md)).
- **A COI rate that gives a minimum age.** The age from the barcodes uses 0.0115 substitutions per site per million years per lineage (about 2.3% pairwise), within the range of rates calibrated on splits of a few million years. Because distances saturate, that age is treated as a lower bound of the crown age. The rate also sets how far back placed sample sequences attach, which is therefore a minimum too, while crown ages are not ([#21](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/21)).
- **TimeTree's genus ages, and a midpoint where TimeTree is unusable.** With `estimate: timetree_midpoint`, where TimeTree dates the species of a genus sampled in the run below the stem cap (and no table bound overrides it), and the barcodes can account for that age ([#54](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/54)), it is used as the crown age. Otherwise, the crown age falls back to the midpoint of the range between the barcode minimum and the upper bound (`rate`) or to the lower estimate (`fraction`). The midpoint is a convention, not a measurement; diversity is reported at both ends of the range for that reason.
- **One representative per BIN** stands in for the variation within it.
- **The genetic code follows from the taxonomy.** The reading-frame check on COI uses a mitochondrial code per record, looked up from its genus, family, order, class or phylum (see [configuration](../technical/configuration.md#genetic-codes)). Records whose names match nothing get a default code, and misidentified records get the code of the wrong group.

| Weakness | Evidence | Issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TimeTree covers marine invertebrates poorly, so many backbone nodes are interpolated. A pure-birth model on the number of genera per clade now places them; it is about as accurate as even spacing and far less sensitive to which genera are sampled, but ignores extinction and rate differences and depends on how finely groups are split into genera | In the current Iberian run 387 of 784 backbone nodes have a TimeTree age (314 directly, 73 through enclosing taxa); 397 are interpolated. On held-out TimeTree ages the median error is a factor 1.30 (1.26 for even spacing); with half the genera dropped, 90% of node ages move by less than a factor 1.20 (1.59) ([results](results/2026-11-interpolation.md)) | [#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| TimeTree crown ages can be far too old (a genus that is not a natural group) or too young (TimeTree knows few of the species asked about). Since [#54](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/54) the crown queried is that of the species sampled, and a crown the barcodes cannot account for is demoted to an upper bound, or not used when far too young; the test allows a genus to evolve up to ten times slower than the rate, so moderately inflated crowns still pass | In the Iberian run 15 TimeTree crowns are demoted (*Haliclona* 444 Ma, *Ruditapes* 69 Ma at 1.3% divergence). Against the run before #54, crowns more than 20 times the barcode age go from 7 to 0 and TimeTree crowns under a third of it from 5 to 0, but 25 genera are still above 10 times (40 before) ([benchmark](../../data/iberia/consistency/benchmark/REPORT.md), [record](../../data/iberia/consistency/README.md)) | [#36](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/36), [#42](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/42), [#54](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/54) |
| TimeTree node-age conflicts in the backbone are resolved by a weighted fit since [#52](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/52); where both sides of a conflict rest on one or two study estimates, the fit settles between them, and re-querying with disjoint picks (#53) classifies only about half of the conflicts | The fit leaves 5 of 387 TimeTree ages more than a factor 1.5 from the final age (26 with the earlier precedence rule), but predicts held-out TimeTree ages no better (median error 1.32 either way). In the Iberian run 13 of 22 re-queried nodes stay unresolved, because TimeTree knows the disjoint picks on one side only or no disjoint picks are left | [#20](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/20), [#52](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/52), [#53](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/53) |

In half of all genera the largest distance within the genus equals the distance to the outgroup. Literature ages for *Gammarus*, *Niphargus* and *Daphnia* are 5 to 12 times the barcode minimum | [#18](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/18) |
| Rates cannot be calibrated on these data. A rate per class fitted to TimeTree crown ages came out two to seven times below published rates and pushed all genera towards one age; marine invertebrates have few fossils, so calibration points will stay scarce | 128 of the 262 genera with a usable TimeTree crown age are teleosts ([record](../../data/iberia/calibration/README.md)) | [#18](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/18), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| Some units do not belong in their genus (misidentification, contamination, NUMTs, or genera that are not natural groups). Screening sets many aside but cannot tell these cases apart, nor which unit of a close pair of different genera has the wrong name; an external reference widens what it compares against, and codon and relative-rate diagnostics flag likely nuclear copies and long branches without acting on them | 1,139 of 24,882 Iberian units set aside (695 closer to another genus, 444 isolated; 1,294 before the search filled in hits crowded out by the first ten); 12 units at least 95% identical to a unit of another order or class ([results](results/2026-11-screening.md)). Of units given the name of another genus of their family, 89% are caught, and about two correct units are set aside for every five caught; 265 units are `numt_like` and 412 are long branches in their genus ([record](../../data/iberia/consistency/README.md#screening-55)) | [#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38), [#55](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/55) |
| Where on its genus's stem a set-aside unit with no close match belongs is not known; halfway is a convention | Raises Faith's PD of samples with five units per genus by 8% | [#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38) |
| Literature crown ages can exceed the stem age in the backbone, and are then cut to 0.9 of it | *Gammarus* (61 Ma against a stem of 53 Ma) and *Niphargus* (87 Ma against 23 Ma) | [#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16), [#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17) |
| The Open Tree of Life contains misplacements. Placement concordance flags a genus when two of three independent checks fail, and re-anchoring moves it to the family of its reference lineage before dating; a wrong match that leaves no second NCBI id behind is mostly listed for review only, a corrected genus returns to its family and not to its place within it, and where OTT pooled the species of two genera, the species of the wrong one still go to TimeTree | The gastropod *Onchidella*, which OTT merged with an echinoderm homonym, is now placed among the molluscs. Of 120 injected merges, 118 are flagged and 117 placed back in their class, none wrongly; 85 of those 117 have a stem age within a factor 1.5 of the one without the error. Of 120 injected name-only errors, 29 are flagged and 91 listed for review ([benchmark](../../data/iberia/consistency/benchmark/REPORT.md)) | [#20](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/20), [#49](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/49), [#50](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/50), [#56](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/56) |
| A genus and its relatives often overlap in barcode distance, so some units end up on the wrong side of the root. Rooting tolerates this, but the root of a genus remains uncertain where many units are misplaced, and keeping more units in their genus (the screening fix of #55) put more of them there | 386 of 1,039 genera have units on the wrong side (median 2, at most 9); 314 units of 188 genera sit among the outgroup, some of them probably misidentified (366 genera and 276 units before the screening fix). 141 genera have more than one plausible root, and the crown age varies by more than 25% over those roots in 19 | [#19](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/19), [#34](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/34), [#75](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/75) |

| BINs and UNITE species hypotheses are delimited differently | PD across markers is comparable in time but not in the granularity of the tips | [#15](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/15) |

## What timegraft is not

It is not a tree of life inferred from barcodes. The deep structure is borrowed from published syntheses; barcodes contribute only the shallow structure and the placement of samples. Results should be presented that way, with the provenance tables that record where every age came from.
