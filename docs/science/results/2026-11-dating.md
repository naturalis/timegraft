# Crown ages: rates, bounds and penalised likelihood (November 2026)

The first real run ([genera of western Iberia](2026-11-iberia.md)) set genus crown ages by a chain of rules: a TimeTree crown age if it passed a threshold, otherwise the barcodes with one substitution rate for all animals. [#36](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/36) asked whether dating with a rate that varies over the tree (penalised likelihood), with outside ages as constraints, would do better. This page reports the tests, including one change that was tried and withdrawn, what was changed in the end, and what that did to the Iberian tree. Scripts and tables are in [`data/iberia/dating-benchmark`](../../../data/iberia/dating-benchmark/README.md).

In short: penalised likelihood does not solve the problem and is not the default. Barcode distances saturate within genera, so they give a minimum crown age, not an estimate, and a rate cannot be calibrated on them. Crown ages are therefore handled as a range, from the barcode minimum to TimeTree and the stem age, with TimeTree's age as the estimate where it has one and the midpoint of the range elsewhere ([#42](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/42)), and diversity is reported at both ends of the range.

> **Since then** ([consistency layer](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/46), [#54](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/54)): the TimeTree crown asked about is that of the species sampled in the run where TimeTree knows two, not of the whole genus, and a TimeTree crown the barcodes cannot account for (more than ten times older than they allow, with saturation) is an upper bound only, while one far too young is not used. The range and the midpoint described here are unchanged. Effect on the Iberian run: crowns more than 20 times the barcode age go from 7 to 0 ([benchmark](../../../data/iberia/consistency/benchmark/REPORT.md)).

## The tests

All 1,046 rooted genus trees of the tolerant Iberian run were rebuilt without dating them, with the code path of the workflow (all 737 rate-based crown ages were reproduced). Penalised likelihood was run with `chronos` from the R package ape (5.7).

### 1. A range on the crown age does not give an estimate

The idea was to give each genus the range of ages allowed by TimeTree and the stem, and let penalised likelihood find the crown age within it. For the 105 genera with a TimeTree crown age accepted by the earlier rule and at least four units, the crown was bounded between half and twice that age, and `chronos` was started three times.

| Model | Oldest over youngest crown age of three starts, median | Above 1.25 | Median difference in penalised log-likelihood between starts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Correlated rates | 1.19 | 21 genera | 0.00006 |
| Relaxed | 1.14 | 26 | 0.19 |
| Strict clock | 1.19 | 6 | 0.000000001 |

The three starts give crown ages that differ by about a fifth and fit equally well. With every tip at the present and only bounds on the root, a tree twice as old with rates half as high explains the branch lengths just as well, so the method has nothing to choose with: where it ends up depends on where it started. Penalised likelihood needs at least one fixed age, and within a genus there is none. The crown age has to be set first, by other means.

### 2. With the crown fixed, smoothing changes little within a genus

On 200 random genera of 4 to 60 units, with the crown fixed, the total branch length of the dated genus tree (its phylogenetic diversity) was compared with that from mean path length.

| Model (smoothing) | PD relative to mean path length, median (5% to 95%) | Differs by more than 10% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strict clock | 0.995 (0.89 to 1.02) | 10 genera |
| Correlated (1) | 1.005 (0.91 to 1.09) | 15 |
| Correlated (0.1) | 1.006 (0.68 to 1.10) | 20 |
| Correlated (10) | 0.988 (0.47 to 1.04) | 44 |
| Relaxed (1) | 0.741 (0.18 to 1.16) | 171 |

- The correlated model, the usual choice, gives the same within-genus PD as mean path length to within 10% in 185 of 200 genera.
- The relaxed model fits a rate to every branch and follows the noise of 658 sites: rates vary as much as their mean, and PD drops by a quarter.
- The information criterion of `chronos` (PHIIC) prefers the strict clock in all 200 genera.
- Stronger smoothing should approach the clock, yet lambda 10 departs from it more often than lambda 1, which points to optimisation trouble on these small, noisy trees.
- Run time is no obstacle: 9, 17 and 27 seconds for genera of 98, 195 and 308 units.

Of all genera, 188 have two or three units, where smoothing has nothing to work with, and 57 have branches of length zero, which `chronos` cannot take and which have to be set to a small value.

### 3. One rate of 0.0115 does not reproduce TimeTree's crown ages

Crown ages from the barcodes were compared with TimeTree crown ages. Two reference sets were used: the 126 TimeTree ages accepted by the earlier rule, which selects in favour of the old rate because it rejected ages more than three times the barcode age; and all 262 TimeTree ages below the stem age, which include ages inflated by genera that are not natural groups. For the recalibrated rates, each genus was left out of its own calibration.

| Estimator | Accepted (126): median estimate over TimeTree | within a factor 2 | All below the stem (262): median | within a factor 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One rate of 0.0115 (as before) | 0.58 | 54% | 0.32 | 26% |
| One recalibrated rate | 1.00 | 84% | 0.99 | 66% |
| Rate from calibrated relatives (family, order or class) | 0.96 | 78% | 0.96 | 68% |
| Rate from the stem: distance to the sister clade over twice the stem age | 2.68 | 37% | 1.92 | 51% |
| Half the stem age, without barcodes | 1.78 | 57% | 1.38 | 70% |

- A rate of 0.0115 gives crown ages about half of TimeTree's even on the set selected in its favour. A slower rate fits better by construction; tests 4 and 5 show why that is not a reason to adopt one.
- Borrowing the rate from the nearest calibrated relatives is no better than one recalibrated rate. The classes with enough data have similar rates, with one clear exception, the elasmobranchs.
- Calibrating a genus on its own stem age fails: the distance to the sister clade is saturated (next point), so crown ages come out two to three times too old.
- On the larger set, half the stem age predicts the TimeTree crown age about as well as the barcodes do. The rank correlation between barcode age and TimeTree age is about 0.5.

### 4. Barcode distances saturate, also within genera

Over 10,440 pairs of a genus and an outgroup unit, the median K2P distance is 0.18 where the backbone puts the divergence at 10 to 20 Ma and 0.27 at 160 to 320 Ma, so the rate implied by the stem falls from 0.0056 to 0.0007 per lineage per million years.

Within genera the picture is the same in a milder form. Over the 262 genera with a TimeTree crown age below the stem age:

| TimeTree crown age (Ma) | Genera | Median root-to-tip distance | Implied rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| below 5 | 25 | 0.040 | 0.0156 |
| 5 to 10 | 26 | 0.070 | 0.0094 |
| 10 to 20 | 84 | 0.074 | 0.0050 |
| 20 to 40 | 76 | 0.091 | 0.0032 |
| 40 to 80 | 33 | 0.110 | 0.0019 |
| above 80 | 18 | 0.128 | 0.0011 |

A twenty-fold range in age corresponds to a three-fold range in distance. Part of this is real: saturation of fast sites, which K2P does not correct for, and the known fall of apparent rates with the age of the calibration (Ho et al. 2005). The rates for the youngest genera are in the range of published COI rates calibrated on splits of about 3 to 3.5 Ma: 1.4% divergence per million years between two lineages in *Alpheus* (Knowlton and Weigt 1998), which is 0.007 per lineage, and 3 to 5% in Arctic molluscs, arthropods and polychaetes (Loeza-Quintana et al. 2019), which is 0.016 to 0.026. Part of it is error in the TimeTree ages, which flattens any such relation. Either way, 80% of all genera have a root-to-tip distance between 0.04 and 0.15, so any constant rate puts their crown ages in a band less than four-fold wide.

### 5. A rate fitted to these data is not a rate

A first attempt at [#18](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/18) calibrated a rate per class as the trimmed median of root-to-tip distance over TimeTree crown age: 0.0043 for teleosts, 0.0041 for gastropods, 0.0029 for malacostracans, 0.0014 for elasmobranchs and 0.0037 pooled ([record](../../../data/iberia/calibration/README.md)). The Iberian tree was rebuilt with these rates, and the result was rejected:

- The median crown age went from 9.4 to 24.8 Ma, and 129 genera reached 0.9 of their stem age, against 5 before. That happened as often where the stem age comes from TimeTree (35 of 347 genera) as where it is interpolated (91 of 683), so the stems are not to blame. The number of genera whose barcode age exceeds 0.9 of the stem age follows the rate: 4 at 0.0115, 26 at 0.007, 161 at 0.0037.
- The fitted rates are two to seven times below published COI rates. Given test 4 this is what a median over genera must give: the distance of a typical genus over the crown age of a typical genus. Every genus is then moved towards that typical age.
- The set is 128 teleosts out of 262 genera, and marine invertebrates will not get many more calibration points.

What outside knowledge says about the two sets of ages:

| | Rate 0.0115 | Fitted rates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Against published COI rates from splits of about 3 Ma (0.007 to 0.026 per lineage) | Within the range | 2 to 7 times slower |
| *Gammarus* (61 Ma in the literature) | 12.9 Ma | 51 Ma |
| *Niphargus* (87 Ma) | 12.1 Ma | 48 Ma |
| *Daphnia* (at least 145 Ma) | 12.2 Ma | 38 Ma |

So a published rate is believable for young genera and gives ages several times too young for old, species-rich ones; the fitted rates do the reverse. Neither is an estimate for all genera. The reason is in the distances themselves: the largest K2P distance within a genus has a median of 0.25, and in half of the genera it is as large as the distance from the genus to its outgroup units (median ratio 0.98). Only 12% of genera have a mean root-to-tip distance below 0.05, where distance still tracks time. With a rate from young splits, the barcode age is a **lower bound**.

### 6. Estimates that need no fitted rate

With a lower bound from the barcodes (at 0.0115) and an upper bound from the stem age (0.9 of it), which age in between agrees best with TimeTree's crown ages? TimeTree itself was not used as a bound here, since it is the reference. Every estimate was kept within the two bounds. Fitted quantities were fitted leaving the genus out.

| Estimator | All below the stem (262): median estimate over TimeTree | within a factor 2 | Genus a clade in the barcode tree (168): median | within a factor 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The barcode minimum | 0.32 | 26% | 0.32 | 26% |
| Half the stem age | 1.39 | 69% | 1.51 | 62% |
| A fitted fraction of the stem age | 1.00 | 71% | 1.02 | 68% |
| Birth model: median crown age given the stem age and the number of units | 2.05 | 49% | 2.13 | 47% |
| **Midpoint: geometric mean of the two bounds** | 0.90 | 75% | 0.96 | 76% |
| Regression on distance | 0.92 | 67% | 0.92 | 70% |
| Regression on distance and stem age | 0.88 | 74% | 0.91 | 74% |
| Regression on distance, stem age and units | 0.88 | 73% | 0.91 | 74% |

- **The birth model fails.** It was the candidate put forward for a rate-free estimate: under pure birth, a clade with a given stem age and number of tips has a known distribution of crown ages. It puts crown ages at 0.42 (two units) to 0.92 (a thousand) of the stem age and is twice too old against TimeTree. It does come close for the three literature genera (*Gammarus* 49 Ma, *Daphnia* 160 Ma), which are old and species-rich, but that is three genera.
- **The midpoint does best, without any fitted quantity.** It is within a factor of two of TimeTree for three quarters of genera and within a factor of three for about nine in ten, on every reference set, including the one selected in favour of the minimum (74% within a factor of two, against 54%).
- **The regression supports it.** The best linear predictor of log crown age is 0.47 log distance + 0.60 log stem age (0.60 and 0.52 for genera that are clades): close to equal weights, which is what a geometric mean is.
- **The stem age alone carries as much as the barcodes.** A fixed fraction of the stem age, which is what the ITS settings use, is within a factor of two for about 70%.

The midpoint has a simple justification that does not rest on this hold-out: if all that is known is that the age lies between a lower and an upper bound, the geometric mean is the choice with the smallest worst-case relative error, the square root of the ratio of the bounds.

### 7. What crown ages do to sample diversity

The total branch length of the tree is a poor guide here. A unit that is the only one of its genus in a sample sits at the end of a path of length equal to the stem age, whatever the crown age. Crown ages only matter where units of the same genus occur together. Random samples on the Iberian tree, with every genus crown at its lower bound, at the midpoint and at its upper bound (200 samples per design):

| Genera x units per genus | PD at the midpoint over PD at the lower bound | At the upper bound | Rank correlation of samples, lower against midpoint | lower against upper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 100 x 1 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 100 x 2 | 1.04 | 1.20 | 1.00 | 0.95 |
| 30 x 3 | 1.06 | 1.26 | 0.99 | 0.87 |
| 300 x 3 | 1.10 | 1.47 | 0.99 | 0.89 |
| 100 x 5 | 1.12 | 1.60 | 0.98 | 0.81 |
| 100 x 20 | 1.28 | 2.36 | 0.90 | 0.66 |

Between the minimum and the midpoint, PD differs by a few per cent to an eighth for samples with up to five units per genus, and samples keep their order. The upper bound, with every genus as old as its stem allows, is an extreme: it adds 20 to 60%, and reorders samples that differ in how many congeners they hold.

## What was changed

1. **A range in place of a rule.** The crown age lies between a lower bound (the barcode age at the marker's rate, raised by a minimum from the literature table) and an upper bound (0.9 of the stem age, the TimeTree crown age, or a maximum from the table). A point age in the table is still used as it is. TimeTree is an upper bound only, because its usual error is an age that is far too old, for genera that are not natural groups. Both bounds are in `provenance.tsv`.
2. **The estimate within the range is a setting.** `crown_age.estimate: minimum` or `midpoint` at first; since [#42](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/42) the default is `timetree_midpoint` (see [the default within the range](#the-default-within-the-range-42)).
3. **Diversity at both ends.** `diversity/alpha.tsv` gives Faith's PD and MPD with every genus at its lower and at its upper bound next to the values for the tree itself.
4. **Rates per taxon** can be set (`crown_age.rates`, `crown_age.rate_table`), for example a slower rate for elasmobranchs, and `python -m timegraft.calibrate` exists, with the warning of test 5.
5. **Literature crown ages** ([#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17)). The table gained a minimum and maximum, a reference and a note, and a first three genera: *Gammarus*, *Niphargus* and *Daphnia* ([`data/crown_ages`](../../../data/crown_ages/README.md)).
6. **Penalised likelihood as an option.** `dating.method: chronos` dates the nodes below a fixed crown. Mean path length stays the default, with LSD2 as the alternative, for the reasons under tests 1 and 2.

## The Iberian run with these changes, at the minimum

Rerun on the same records, units, backbone and rooting, with the rate at 0.0115 and the estimate at the minimum. These are the numbers before units were screened ([#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38)); screening lowered the median crown age to 8.2 Ma and is reported [separately](2026-11-screening.md).

| Crown age | Genera | Median (Ma) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The barcode minimum, within all bounds | 1,014 | 9.0 |
| Lowered to the TimeTree age | 25 | 4.4 |
| Lowered to 0.9 of the stem age | 4 | 7.0 |
| Raised to the literature minimum (*Daphnia*) | 1 | 145 |
| Literature age, cut to 0.9 of the stem age (*Gammarus*, *Niphargus*) | 2 | |

- **Close to the earlier run.** The median crown age is 8.9 Ma (9.4 before), and the total branch length 329,193 million years (312,011). The difference is mainly the 126 genera that took a TimeTree age before and now take the barcode minimum, and the three literature genera.
- **The range is wide.** The upper bound is the stem age for 788 genera, TimeTree for 256 and the table for 2. It is a median of 6.5 times the lower bound, more than ten times in 371 genera and less than twice in 123. With every crown at the midpoint, the median crown age would be 21.5 Ma and the total branch length 534,263 million years; at the upper bound, 57.2 Ma and 1,291,571.
- **No jumps.** *Idas* ranges from 9.0 to 11.2 Ma over its plausible roots (9.0 to 30.9 Ma under the threshold), within bounds of 9.0 to 30.9 Ma. Over all genera with a barcode-based crown age, the age varies by more than a quarter over the plausible roots in 160 of 1,044.
- **The literature ages do not fit the backbone.** *Gammarus* (61 Ma, stem 53 Ma) and *Niphargus* (87 Ma, stem 23 Ma) are cut to 0.9 of the stem age. For these two, the backbone node is too young or the published age too old ([#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16)).

## The default within the range (#42)

The minimum was first kept as the default, pending a decision. Two observations settled it.

- **Genera that TimeTree dates were short-changed.** Where TimeTree gives a crown age below the stem, that age is also the upper bound. With the minimum, those 260 genera came out at a median of 0.32 of TimeTree's age; even the midpoint between the minimum and TimeTree is only 0.56 of it. TimeTree aggregates the published timetrees, and nothing derived here from saturated barcodes is better evidence. So where TimeTree gives an age below the stem, it is used as the crown age.
- **Elsewhere the midpoint is the best estimate available.** For the other 71% of genera there is nothing to aggregate. The midpoint between the barcode minimum and 0.9 of the stem age was within a factor of two of TimeTree for three quarters of the genera TimeTree does date (test 6), the minimum for a quarter.

`crown_age.estimate: timetree_midpoint` does this and is now the default. Literature ages are not pursued to fill the gaps ([#17](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/17)): TimeTree is already queried thoroughly, and where it has no data, hand-curated ages would not scale.

TimeTree ages are taken as they are, without a threshold, because thresholds made ages jump. In 23 genera TimeTree's age is more than ten times the barcode minimum. They are mostly demosponges (7), malacostracans (4) and elasmobranchs and chimaeras (4), groups with slowly evolving mitochondrial DNA, where a barcode minimum is expected to be far too young. A few are probably inflated by genera that are not natural groups, *Haliclona* above all (444 Ma, against a stem of 519 Ma). `provenance.tsv` has both ages (`timetree_crown`, `barcode_age`) for anyone who wants to check.

Rebuilt from the backbone onwards, with the pure-birth interpolation of undated backbone nodes ([#16](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/16), [results](2026-11-interpolation.md)) and unit screening ([#38](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/38)); 45 minutes on two cores.

- The median crown age is 24.8 Ma (8.2 Ma at the minimum), and the total branch length of the tree 638,002 Ma (360,668 Ma with every crown at its lower bound, 1,424,818 Ma at its upper bound).
- Only one genus reaches 0.9 of its stem age, against 129 with the rates fitted in test 5: the midpoint cannot reach the cap.

## What this means

- Barcodes bound crown ages from below; they do not estimate them. This holds for any marker that saturates, and more so for k-mer distances on ITS.
- A general scheme follows from that, and it needs no calibration points: a lower bound from the marker where a rate from young splits exists, an upper bound from the stem age and TimeTree, TimeTree's age where it has one, the midpoint elsewhere, and diversity reported at both ends of the range. Where there is no rate, there is no lower bound, and the estimate is TimeTree's age or else a fraction of the stem age, as for ITS.
- For samples with few units per genus the choice within the range hardly matters for PD. For samples rich in congeners the range is wide, and results should be reported with it.
- Better ages now depend on better syntheses, TimeTree above all, more than on anything done with barcodes. Within-genus PD is set by the crown age; what happens below it changes PD by a few per cent.
