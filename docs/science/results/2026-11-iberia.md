# First real run: genera of western Iberia (November 2026)

The first run on real data, to see whether the pipeline works at scale and where it breaks. Numbers are from the run with the crown age checks and conflict handling of PR #13.

## Data

BOLD public data (the March 2026 data package, as Parquet), cut down in two steps with `timegraft.bcdm`:

1. Records of Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Cnidaria, Bryozoa, Porifera and Chordata from Portugal and Spain, excluding insects, arachnids, springtails, millipedes, centipedes and tetrapods, to find the genera present in the region.
2. All COI-5P records of those genera worldwide, at most ten per BIN.

Result: 102,794 records, 1,232 genera, 25,578 BINs. Phyla by records: Arthropoda 30,612, Mollusca 28,345, Annelida 20,473, Chordata 19,070, Porifera 1,254, Cnidaria 1,206, Echinodermata 932, Bryozoa 888, plus a few algae. The largest genera were *Niphargus* (1,035 BINs), *Gammarus* (907), *Proasellus* (389), *Alpheus* (308) and *Fridericia* (282).

The country filter brought in far more than coastal marine taxa: groundwater amphipods, earthworms and enchytraeids, land snails, *Daphnia*. For the CBMA project, a list of genera from the samples themselves is the better filter.

## Settings

Backbone built from OToL release files (opentree16.1, OTT 3.7.3; context Metazoa) and TimeTree; COI aligned with MAFFT per genus; K2P; BioNJ; crown ages from TimeTree when they pass the checks, otherwise from a rate of 0.0115 substitutions per site per Myr per lineage; mean path length dating; 8 shards on 2 cores.

## Results

- **Run time**: 20 to 28 minutes (representatives about 5 minutes, backbone about 10 on the first run, genus subtrees about 13).
- **Tree**: 24,894 tips, ultrametric, root at 750 Ma.
- **Records**: 725 rejected, nearly all for too many ambiguous bases.
- **Units**: 24,882 assigned to a genus; of the 215 without a usable genus, 212 were placed next to their nearest unit, 3 stayed unplaced.
- **Genera in the backbone**: 996 are nodes in the synthesis, 149 attached at the MRCA of their species, 38 at an enclosing taxon, 32 unmatched in OTT, 3 ambiguous; 35 genera (200 units) did not make it into the backbone.
- **Genus subtrees**: 1,046, plus 137 single-unit genera.

### Backbone node ages

| Source | Nodes |
| --- | --- |
| TimeTree, direct | 261 |
| TimeTree, via enclosing taxa | 53 |
| Interpolated | 332 |
| Interpolated, TimeTree does not resolve the node | 57 |
| Interpolated after a conflicting TimeTree age was dropped | 76 |

Of the 76 conflicts, 15 have a TimeTree age more than 1.5 times that of the dated ancestor. The largest involve flatfishes (*Bathysolea*, *Buglossidium*), crabs (*Carcinus*, *Callinectes*) and freshwater snails (*Lymnaea*, *Ancylus*). One is a clear error in OTT: the gastropod *Onchidella* sits among the sea urchins, under Laganidae, because OTT merged it with an echinoderm homonym.

Rejected TimeTree estimates included *Haliclona* (444 Ma), *Stenasellus* (289), *Margaritifera* (230), *Sipunculus* (208) and *Hydra* (193): MRCAs of every species TimeTree knows under these names. Accepted TimeTree crown ages have a median of 12.4 Ma, rate-based ones 9.5 Ma. Before the checks, the TimeTree median was 21 Ma.

### Rooting

852 genus subtrees were rooted on the sister-genus outgroup; in 194 (19%) the outgroup fell inside the ingroup and midpoint rooting was used.

### Genetic code per record

The run above used one genetic code, the invertebrate mitochondrial code (5), for the reading-frame check. Filtering the same 102,794 records again with a code per record ([#22](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/22)) changed the outcome of 3 records:

| Code | Source | Records | Rejected for stop codons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | mapping | 80,318 | 9 |
| 2 | mapping | 19,070 | 5 |
| 4 | mapping | 2,465 | 0 |
| 9 | mapping | 932 | 0 |
| 1 | mapping | 9 | 0 |

- Every record got its code from the mapping; none fell back to the default. All 19,070 chordates are fishes (tetrapods were excluded) and resolved to code 2, mostly through their order and the rest through BOLD's class Teleostei. Sponges (code 4) resolved through their orders, because Porifera as a whole is mixed in NCBI. The red algae (*Gigartinales*) got 4 and the kelps (*Laminariales*) 1.
- The 3 newly rejected records are fishes (*Hygophum*, *Pomadasys*, *Sphyraena*), each with an AGA or AGG within the first 25 codons of the best frame. In *Sphyraena* the protein reads correctly again just after the stop (GDQIYNVI, against GDDQIYNVI in the conserved motif), which points to an indel near the start of the read rather than a pseudogene. All other records in their three BINs pass, so no unit was lost.
- Total rejections went from 724 to 727, nearly all for ambiguous bases (713). Stop codons account for 11 rejections with one code and 14 with a code per record, probably because BOLD already flags records with stop codons.

The per-record code matters little for the stop-codon check on BOLD data, then. Its value lies in correct translation for amino-acid based checks such as NUMT detection, and in data that BOLD has not screened.

## What this tells us

- The architecture holds at this scale: everything runs in under half an hour, and every age has a recorded source.
- TimeTree dates a little over a third of backbone nodes directly; the rest depends on interpolation, which is the weakest part of the backbone.
- TimeTree crown ages need checking, and the check depends on the assumed COI rate, which itself needs calibrating.
- Conflicts between TimeTree and OToL are a useful way to find errors in either.
- The rooting failure rate is high enough to investigate.

These points are tracked as issues.
