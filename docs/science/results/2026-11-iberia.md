# First real run: genera of western Iberia (November 2026)

The first run on real data, to see whether the pipeline works at scale and where it breaks. Numbers are from the run with the crown age checks and conflict handling of PR #13.

> **Crown ages have changed since.** The sections on genus crown ages below describe the earlier rule (a TimeTree age accepted or rejected at a threshold, one rate of 0.0115). Crown ages are now an age from the barcodes within bounds, with a rate per class, and are almost three times older; the tree's total branch length is 77% larger. See [crown ages: rates, bounds and penalised likelihood](2026-11-dating.md). Data, backbone, units and rooting are unchanged, and the archive in `data/iberia/` holds the new run.

## Data

BOLD public data (the March 2026 data package, as Parquet), cut down in two steps with `timegraft.bcdm`:

1. Records of Annelida, Mollusca, Arthropoda, Echinodermata, Cnidaria, Bryozoa, Porifera and Chordata from Portugal and Spain, excluding insects, arachnids, springtails, millipedes, centipedes and tetrapods, to find the genera present in the region.
2. All COI-5P records of those genera worldwide, at most ten per BIN.

Result: 102,794 records, 1,232 genera, 25,166 BINs. Phyla by records: Arthropoda 30,612, Mollusca 28,345, Annelida 20,473, Chordata 19,070, Porifera 1,254, Cnidaria 1,206, Echinodermata 932, Bryozoa 888, plus a few algae. The largest genera were *Niphargus* (1,035 BINs), *Gammarus* (907), *Proasellus* (389), *Alpheus* (308) and *Fridericia* (282).

The country filter brought in far more than coastal marine taxa: groundwater amphipods, earthworms and enchytraeids, land snails, *Daphnia*. For the CBMA project, a list of genera from the samples themselves is the better filter.

## Settings

Backbone built from OToL release files (opentree16.1, OTT 3.7.3; context Metazoa) and TimeTree; COI aligned with MAFFT per genus; K2P; BioNJ; crown ages from TimeTree when they pass the checks, otherwise from a rate of 0.0115 substitutions per site per Myr per lineage; mean path length dating; 8 shards on 2 cores.

## Results

- **Run time**: 20 to 28 minutes (representatives about 5 minutes, backbone about 10 on the first run, genus subtrees about 13).
- **Tree**: 24,894 tips, ultrametric, root at 750 Ma.
- **Records**: 725 rejected, nearly all for too many ambiguous bases.
- **Units**: 24,882 assigned to a genus; of the 215 without a usable genus, 212 were placed next to their nearest unit, 3 stayed unplaced.
- **Genera in the backbone**: 996 are nodes in the synthesis, 149 attached at the MRCA of their species, 38 at an enclosing taxon, 32 unmatched in OTT, 3 ambiguous; 35 genera (200 units) did not make it into the backbone.
- **Genus subtrees**: 1,046, plus 137 single-unit genera.

### Rooting

852 genus subtrees were rooted on the sister-genus outgroup; in 194 (19%) the outgroup fell inside the ingroup and midpoint rooting was used.

#### Why rooting fails

A clean rerun on 10 November 2026 (outputs in `data/iberia/`) reproduced every rooting, and `timegraft.rooting_diagnostics` examined all 1,046 subtrees ([#19](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/19)).

- **The genus and its sister genus are not separable by distance.** In a logistic regression on genus size, the largest distance within the genus, the smallest distance from the genus to the outgroup and stem age, only the two distances matter (z = +8.1 and −7.4); genus size and stem age do not (z = −0.1 and −1.5). When the nearest outgroup unit is farther from the genus than the two most distant units of the genus are from each other, rooting failed in 2 of 330 genera (0.6%); otherwise in 192 of 716 (27%).
- **It is not the backbone, saturation or short sequences.** Failure rates hardly differ by backbone status (18% for genera in the synthesis, 20% attached at the MRCA of their species, 24% at an enclosing taxon). No distance between genus and outgroup reached the saturation cap, the oldest quarter of genera failed least (11%), and K2P with gamma-distributed rates (α = 0.5) rescued 8 of 194. Outgroup units in failed and successful genera had the same length (median 648 and 649 sites).
- **Which two units are drawn decides much of it.** Twenty random pairs of units from the first sister genus, per genus, failed 21% of the time, close to the observed 19%. For genera that failed in the run, 41% of random pairs worked, and only 9 of 163 failed with every pair; of the genera that succeeded, 194 of 597 failed with some pairs.
- **The test, not the outgroup, is the weak point.** Rooting requires every outgroup unit to fall outside the genus, so more outgroup units make failure more likely: with five or ten units spread over the sister genera, 12 to 17 of the 194 genera passed. Yet the best root then had a median of only two units on the wrong side.
- **The fallback costs little PD.** On the 764 genera with at least three units that were rooted on the outgroup, midpoint and MAD (minimal ancestor deviation) found the same root as the outgroup in 49% and 55%. The crown age from barcodes and rate moved by more than 25% in 58 genera with midpoint (median ratio 0.97) and 50 with MAD (1.00); relative PD within the genus moved by more than 25% in 39 and 36. Agreement with the outgroup did not fall with stem age.

#### Would more distant outgroups help?

Outgroups farther out were compared with the sister genus on 300 simulated genera with known roots and on Iberian genera (benchmark in `data/iberia/outgroup-benchmark/`). On the Iberian data, the reference root was the run's own outgroup root in the 408 genera where every one of 20 random sister-genus pairs gave a clean split; another random pair from the sister genus found the same root in 74%, which is the attainable level. All methods shared one alignment per genus.

| outgroup | simulation: true root | Iberia: reference root | Iberia, failed genera: passes the test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| run's sister-genus pair | 51% | (reference) | 2 of 184 |
| another sister-genus pair | 52% | 74% | 57 of 131 |
| two nearest units beyond the genus's own spread, from up to four sister clades | 59%, no better than the sister pair on the same genera | 61% | 101 of 113 |
| two random units from other families of the order | 45% | 63% | 93 of 163 |
| two nearest units from other families of the order | 38% | 64% | 101 of 163 |
| ten units over the sister clades, minimum-violation root | 54% | 69% | all |
| MAD, no outgroup | 48% | 54% | all |
| midpoint, no outgroup | 46% | 51% | all |

- **Other families are worse.** In the simulation they found the true root significantly less often than the run's own outgroup (paired sign test, p = 0.006 and < 0.001) and moved the crown age by more than 25% in a quarter of genera. On the Iberian data they also did worse than another sister-genus pair (p ≤ 0.001). In K2P they are hardly farther away than the sister genus (median 0.24 against 0.21): COI distances barely grow beyond the genus. Also, 39% of the backbone's sister genera are already in another family by BOLD's taxonomy.
- **Picking units beyond the genus's spread passes the test** (101 of 113 failed genera) but does not root better: no better than the sister pair in the simulation, and worse on the Iberian data (61% against 75%, p < 0.001). Passing the test is not the same as finding the root.
- **The tolerant rule roots every genus at no loss of accuracy:** 54% in the simulation, against 51% for the run's outgroup, and 72% against 74% for another sister-genus pair on the Iberian data (paired, not significant).

So rooting should accept a few misplaced units rather than demand a clean split: a minimum-violation root on several outgroup units from more than one sister genus, with MAD as the fallback, recording the misplaced units (some will be misassigned BINs) in the provenance.

#### With the tolerant rule

This is now the default (ten outgroup units from the nearest three sister clades; [#19](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/19)). Rerun on the same records and backbone, every genus subtree was rooted on its outgroup: 512 with a clean split and 534 with units on the wrong side, a median of 2 (at most 9, at most 29% of the genus and its outgroup). In 351 genera, 579 units of the genus fell among the outgroup; they stay in the tree as the earliest branches of their genus and are listed in `provenance.tsv` for checking. Crown ages changed little: the median ratio to the strict run is 1.00, and 59 genera moved by more than 25% (30 of the 852 rooted on the outgroup before, 29 of the 194 that fell back to midpoint). Total PD of the tree changed by −0.2%. The run took 36 minutes instead of 32.

#### How uncertain the root remains

`provenance.tsv` now records, per genus, how uncertain the root is and what that does to the crown age and PD ([#34](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/34); columns in [running](../../technical/running.md#outputs)). Rerunning the tolerant run with these columns gave the same outputs as before (the archive in `data/iberia/` now holds the rerun), plus:

- Root positions within one violation of the best: a single one in 792 genera, two or more in 254, three or more in 114 (at most 11).
- MAD's root ambiguity index (954 genera with at least three units): median 0.93, at least 0.9 in 547. Most genus trees have another MAD root that is about as good, so the index separates genera less well than the count above.
- Crown ages from the barcodes (920 genera): the largest over the smallest across the alternatives has a median of 1.03; it exceeds 1.25 in 149 genera, 1.5 in 76 and 2 in 36, and is exactly 1 in 387. The smallest age is below the chosen one in 422 genera, mostly because leaving out the misplaced units makes the genus younger. At the upper end the TimeTree check can turn: a younger or older barcode age decides whether the TimeTree age is accepted, which is what makes *Idas* range from 9.0 to 30.9 Ma.
- Within-genus PD: no change in 541 genera, a relative range above 0.1 in 86 and above 0.25 in 26 (median 0, 90th percentile 0.08).

The extra work takes about 3% of the CPU time of the subtree step, most of it MAD on the two largest genera (*Niphargus*, 1,033 units, 21 s; *Gammarus*, 905). One alternative root (*Nereiphylla*) gave a negative crown age, from negative BioNJ branch lengths, and such roots are now skipped.

### Genetic code per record

The run above used one genetic code, the invertebrate mitochondrial code (5), for the reading-frame check. Filtering the same 102,794 records again with a code per record ([#22](https://github.com/naturalis/timegraft/issues/22)) changed the outcome of 3 records:

| Code | Source | Records | Rejected for stop codons |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | mapping | 80,318 | 9 |
| 2 | mapping | 19,070 | 5 |
| 4 | mapping | 2,465 | 0 |
| 9 | mapping | 932 | 0 |
| 1 | mapping | 9 | 0 |

- Every record got its code from the mapping; none fell back to the default. All 19,070 chordates are fishes (tetrapods were excluded) and resolved to code 2, mostly through their order and the rest through BOLD's class Teleostei. Sponges (code 4) resolved through their orders, because Porifera as a whole is mixed in NCBI. The red algae (*Gigartinales*) got 4 and the kelps (*Laminariales*) 1.
- The 3 newly rejected records are fishes (*Hygophum*, *Pomadasys*, *Sphyraena*), each with an AGA or AGG within the first 25 codons of the best frame. In *Sphyraena* the protein reads correctly again just after the stop (GDQIYNVI, against GDDQIYNVI in the conserved motif), which points to an indel near the start of the read rather than a pseudogene. All other records in their three BINs pass, so no unit was lost.
- Total rejections went from 724 to 727, nearly all for ambiguous bases (713). Stop codons account for 11 rejections with one code and 14 with a code per record, probably because BOLD already flags records with stop codons.

The per-record code matters little for the stop-codon check on BOLD data, then. Its value lies in correct translation for amino-acid based checks such as NUMT detection, and in data that BOLD has not screened.

## What this tells us

- The architecture holds at this scale: everything runs in about half an hour, and every age has a recorded source.
- TimeTree dates a little over a third of backbone nodes directly; the rest depends on interpolation, which is the weakest part of the backbone.
- TimeTree crown ages need checking, and the check depends on the assumed COI rate, which itself needs calibrating.
- Conflicts between TimeTree and OToL are a useful way to find errors in either.
- Rooting fails where a genus and its sister genus overlap in barcode distance; a rooting rule that tolerates a few misplaced units should fix most of it.

These points are tracked as issues.
